# Feasibility study summary

Structural arbitrage is mechanically testable, but apparent opportunities may be
short-lived, competitive, too shallow, or erased by fees, rounding, latency, and
legging risk. Binary complete sets are therefore a practical first research target,
not guaranteed profit.

Vanilla NegRisk is attractive only when machine-readable structure supports a formal
terminal-state model. Ambiguous or augmented events stay excluded. Complete-set
Holding Rewards are an empirical hypothesis: eligibility, parameters, operational
risk, and actual credits require observation and reconciliation. Market making is
technically feasible but adds inventory and adverse-selection risk; incentives must
not mask losing trading. LLM directional betting is not the safe core, and future
LLM relationship suggestions require deterministic formal validation.

No strategy is known to be risk-free or guaranteed profitable. Version-sensitive
fees, rewards, limits, and mechanics must be recorded as dated observations from
current official sources rather than frozen here.
